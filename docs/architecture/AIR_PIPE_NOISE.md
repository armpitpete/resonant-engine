# Air / Pipe / Noise — Sonic Design Contract (M0.2)

Status: **APPROVED FOR M0**

Air / pipe / noise is a primary sonic design priority for Resonant Engine. This document is normative for architecture and later sound design.

## Core idea

Air is treated as **energy and excitation**, not as a decorative noise layer placed on top of a pitched sound.

A successful continuously excited resonant voice must be able to move through changing regimes as energy, interaction, tuning, damping and feedback change. Static presets are secondary to transition behavior.

## Definitions

### Air

A broad audible result of moving excitation energy through a system. Air may contain noise, pressure fluctuations, resonant coloration and partial pitch, but it is not defined as white noise.

### Pressure

A continuous control representing available driving force or excitation potential. Pressure may influence excitation amplitude, interaction strength, flow, feedback conditions or other model-specific behavior. It is not required to map linearly to output level.

### Turbulence

Irregular broadband or band-limited excitation generated by an interaction or flow process. Turbulence may be stochastic, deterministic-chaotic or shaped noise-like energy. It can remain audible without stable pitch.

### Continuous excitation

Excitation that can persist and change at audio/control timescales without requiring a note-triggered burst. Breath, bowing, rubbing, flow and driven feedback systems are examples.

### Transient excitation

A finite event such as an impulse, click, strike, pluck, burst or short injection of energy.

### Resonator interaction

Excitation and resonator state may affect one another. Returned resonator energy may alter the exciter rather than the exciter being a permanently one-way source.

### Passive resonance

A resonator that loses net energy when external excitation stops.

### Regenerative resonance

A resonator in which returned energy extends or strengthens resonance without necessarily becoming indefinitely self-sustaining.

### Self-sustaining resonance

A regime in which the system can maintain audible oscillation or activity because active feedback/excitation compensates for losses.

### Unstable resonance

A musically usable regime where amplitude, pitch, spectrum or mode dominance becomes irregular, bifurcated, overblown, beating or noise-like. This is distinct from numerical failure.

### Overblow

A transition in which increased excitation/interaction causes a resonant system to reorganize toward a higher mode, register, oscillation pattern or otherwise more energetic regime. Resonant Engine does not require one acoustic-instrument definition of overblow.

### Noise-to-pitch transition

A continuous change from predominantly broadband/irregular excitation toward increasingly coherent resonant periodicity or modal dominance.

### Pitch-to-noise transition

A continuous loss of stable periodic/modal organization toward irregular, turbulent, chaotic or broadband behavior.

## Physical plausibility and impossible behavior

Resonant Engine must support models that are physically plausible enough to evoke familiar acoustic behavior, but physical realism is optional rather than mandatory.

Deliberately impossible systems are valid when their behavior remains coherent and expressive: impossible material loss, active energy injection, synthetic coupling, non-acoustic feedback paths, extreme dispersion and unusual resonator networks are all legitimate future territory.

A model is not judged solely by whether it matches a real object. It is judged by whether the energy, interaction and transitions make a musically useful instrument.

## Why transitions matter

A preset that produces a convincing static pipe tone is insufficient. The engine must support convincing movement between regimes.

The required future continuum is:

```text
silence
→ faint air
→ turbulence
→ emerging pitch
→ unstable resonance
→ stable pipe
→ rich pipe
→ overblow
→ aggressive resonance
→ noise
```

No M0 abstraction may make any segment of that continuum architecturally impossible.

## Breath Pipe Reference Voice

The Breath Pipe Reference Voice is the protected architectural test voice for M0. It is not yet an implementation requirement and it is not a Steampipe clone.

```text
Pressure + Turbulence
        ↓
Exciter interaction
        ↕
Tuned resonator
        ↺
Active feedback
        ↓
Feedback filtering
        ↓
Nonlinearity
        ↺
```

The arrows are conceptual. A later model may implement the behavior using a waveguide, modal system, tuned delay, coupled structure or hybrid method.

## Required future expressive controls

The architecture must be able to support continuous control of at least these conceptual dimensions, even if final parameter names differ by model:

- pressure / drive energy;
- turbulence amount and character;
- excitation interaction strength;
- resonator tuning;
- damping/loss;
- feedback amount and polarity;
- feedback spectral shaping;
- nonlinear interaction/drive;
- dispersion or stiffness where relevant;
- coupling where relevant;
- external excitation mix;
- expressive pitch and per-note pressure/expression.

Controls may interact nonlinearly. They are not required to behave like independent subtractive-synth knobs.

## Required continuous behavior

A future Breath Pipe must be able to:

- remain truly silent at zero energy;
- produce faint unpitched air before stable tone;
- create turbulence without forcing pitch;
- let pitch emerge from resonant interaction;
- cross unstable regions without numerical failure;
- settle into stable resonance;
- enrich into stronger nonlinear resonance;
- cross into overblow-like states;
- become aggressive and eventually noise-dominant;
- reverse direction through those regimes without discontinuous architecture changes.

## Sonic acceptance vocabulary

Use these terms during listening/evaluation rather than vague `good/bad` alone:

- **silent** — no unintended floor or hidden oscillator;
- **airy** — audible excitation with weak/no stable pitch;
- **turbulent** — irregular broadband energy is a meaningful part of the sound;
- **emergent** — pitch develops from interaction rather than appearing as a switched oscillator;
- **stable** — dominant resonance is coherent and controllable;
- **regenerative** — feedback audibly extends/builds resonant energy;
- **self-sustaining** — resonance persists through active energy balance;
- **unstable** — musically irregular but numerically valid;
- **overblown** — excitation causes a register/mode/state transition;
- **aggressive** — high-energy nonlinear/resonant behavior remains controllable;
- **collapsed** — desired resonant behavior has disappeared unintentionally;
- **runaway** — numerical or amplitude growth exceeds defined safety behavior;
- **dead** — model is technically stable but lacks expressive response to movement;
- **stepped** — a supposedly continuous transition exposes parameter/block discontinuity.

## Instrument criterion

A technically respectable physical model that produces a bad or unresponsive instrument is insufficient.

M0 and later milestones should preserve the ability to optimize for playability, expressive transitions, controllable instability and useful impossible behavior rather than treating physical accuracy as the only success criterion.
